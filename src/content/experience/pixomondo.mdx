## Two jobs that were really one

On paper this role was two things: design the campaigns, and look after the website. In
practice they were a single pipeline with a deadline attached to someone else's release
schedule.

A visual effects studio markets against the films and series it has worked on, so each campaign
was pinned to a release date that wasn't going to move. The work ran print and digital together.
**Art for the trade press, a matching hero update on the site, and a blog post to land
alongside them.**

The same spreads went out to *Variety*, *Cinefex* and *Blickpunkt* (the general industry
paper, the visual effects journal, and the German market). Each piece had to hold up in
three different editorial contexts without being redrawn for each.

## The site was the bottleneck

The existing website couldn't carry the campaign on the timeline available. So the redesign
wasn't a separate initiative that happened to coincide. It was a dependency. If the site
wasn't rebuilt, the ads had nowhere to point.

I met regularly with the CEO to agree the design direction, then built it: a LEMP stack
(CentOS, NGINX, MySQL, PHP) with a **WordPress theme written from scratch** rather than adapted
from a template, so the campaign layouts could be changed each month without fighting someone
else's assumptions.

Visits rose 10%, and the campaign launched on time.

## Print doesn't let you iterate

This is the only work I've done that couldn't be changed after it shipped. No analytics, no
patch, no second version. It goes to press at full size and that's the artifact. You make
the decisions before it exists rather than after, which is a different discipline from the
one screens teach.
