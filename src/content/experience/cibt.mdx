## Decisions

<p class="why">Analytics showed where people left, not why.</p>

**Ask where people leave.** I put polls directly on the two pages losing people: the
requirements page, where travelers check what a visa needs, and the application funnel. Round
one returned **420 responses**, 291 on requirements and 129 in the funnel, but the answers
were vague.

<p class="why">An open question asks for a thought before someone has agreed to take part.</p>

**Lead with yes or no.** For round two I opened with a binary question and asked the open
one only as a follow-up. **One week of the redesigned survey drew more than double what five
weeks of the original had**, and the answers got more specific: people who had found what they
needed and hit a particular wall.

<p class="why">Hundreds of free-text answers don't rank themselves.</p>

**Code the answers into themes.** I sorted the free text into categories, which put the
blockers in order.

<p class="dv-cap">Mentions, coded from open-text responses on the requirements page</p>

**Unclear requirements** was the largest category by far, ahead of processing time, price, and
not being able to find documents like letters of invitation. Underneath them sat a request the
site had no answer for: *let me save this and come back*. People gathered passports, photos and
employer letters over days, and the funnel assumed a single sitting.
