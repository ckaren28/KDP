## What travelers told me

I sat with the travel manager to see how requests were really processed, and interviewed the
employees who traveled most.

- Trips had several legs, and the old form assumed a simple out-and-back.
- Travelers weren't sure which time zone to enter times in.
- Changing a request meant starting over.
- Requests needed a manager's approval, and a way to comment and resubmit.

<figure class="stage">
  <WetWorkflow />

## One request, several legs

<p class="why">Heard: trips had several legs, and time zones confused people.</p>

The form holds as many legs as the trip needs, single and round trips together. Each leg says
which location's time to use, and airports come from a code lookup instead of free text.

## A request that lives

<p class="why">Heard: changing a request meant starting over, and every trip needed a manager's approval.</p>

Managers approve or decline from an email that carries the trip's essentials. A request can be
commented on, updated and resubmitted instead of started over.

## Two views, two jobs

<p class="why">Heard: the travel manager was absorbing everything over email.</p>

Employees see their own requests. The travel manager sees every request in one table, with
approve and decline right there. Red is the employee side; blue is the manager's.
