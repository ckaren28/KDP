## The system before

Three things were wrong with the app it replaced, and only one of them was visual.

**It couldn't be maintained.** The person who built it had left, taking the knowledge with
them. The documentation that remained was close to useless. Changes that should have been
routine weren't happening at all.

**It couldn't be adjusted.** Altering a form field or adding an email setting was
disproportionately difficult, so the form drifted further from what the travel manager
actually needed with every passing month.

**It couldn't be reached.** The app was desktop-only. At a company whose employees spend
their days building fountains in the field rather than sitting behind a desk, that made the
tool useless at exactly the moment someone needed it.

## Finding the actual objective

The brief I was handed was "replace the legacy travel management system." That's a
description of the work, not a goal, so I interviewed the travel manager and the employees
filing requests before designing anything.

Two things came out of it that weren't in the brief. The travel manager needed **strong
admin functionality**, because she was the one absorbing every gap in the old form as
manual follow-up. And employees needed to **add context to a request**, because a trip to a job
site isn't a set of dropdowns and the old form forced them to file something not quite true
and then explain it over email.

That reframed the project: not a port, but an intuitive, streamlined app with real admin
tooling behind it.

## Designing it inside someone else's brand

WET had a large design department and a comprehensive brand book. The app was internal, but
aligning it with the brand was a stated objective, since internal tools are still the
company talking to its own staff.

I worked from the primary brand colors, red and black, and **added pink as an accent**, which isn't in the book. Pink reads as warm and happy, it's a hue of the brand red so it
sits inside the existing system rather than fighting it, and against travel it carries a
useful note of urgency and play. It let an internal utility feel like it belonged to the
company without looking like a form.

## What shipped

An intuitive, modern app that worked on both desktop and mobile, built in Vue with SASS and
GraphQL. A manager's home page for the admin side, and a request form that finally let
people say what they actually needed.

The old system was phased out.

## What I'd do differently

I inherited an app that died with its author and then built its replacement alone, in a
month, at a company I left five months later. I documented the design decisions but not the
system, which means I handed over the same problem I'd been hired to fix, just newer.
